Edwin Harrow's *The Lamplighter's Almanac* (Greyfield Press, 1998). A short first-person memoir-like narrative in three chapters: the narrator spent eleven months in the harbour town of Port Kessel and recalls the old lamplighter Tobias Wren, his lamps and the family almanacs. Quiet, precise British prose (harbour, favour, grey); the author's six endnotes are factual asides in the same voice.

Register: plain, restrained literary Chinese, like a good translation of English essay-memoir — 王佐良、董桥 translating English prose, or 杨苡's quieter passages: short sentences, concrete nouns, no ornament the English does not have. The book's effect is understatement; do not make it more sentimental, more lyrical or more explanatory than the English. Avoid 四字成语 piled up for colour, avoid colloquial internet Chinese, avoid 文言.

Who speaks: the narrator is 我 throughout. Tobias's speech is short, dry, a little gnomic; render it as natural spoken Chinese of an old working man — simple words, no dialect, no 啦/呗. His aphorisms ("It keeps me early", "It's the only lamp that says no") should stay short and slightly odd, as in English; do not smooth them into proverbs.

Names: 新华社 transliteration for invented English names; keep each name identical everywhere (glossary is authoritative). Tobias Wren → 托拜厄斯·雷恩 (later just 托拜厄斯). Port Kessel → 凯塞尔港; Kessel alone → 凯塞尔. Rope Street → 绳索街. Skerra → 斯凯拉岛 (the island). Ship names are titled in italics in English: render the ship *Margaret Ellen* as “玛格丽特·埃伦号”. The book's own title is 《点灯人的历书》; "almanac(s)" is always 历书.

Italics: three uses here. (1) A foreign word — *saudade* (Portuguese): keep the word in Latin letters, without parentheses or gloss, because the text explains it right after. (2) A phrase or word the characters used as a term (*the turning*, *CLEAR*): put the Chinese in quotation marks “ ” in place of the italics. (3) Words written in the almanac (*Fog. Mother died at four. …*): translate them, set off by a full-width colon and quotation marks “ ”. CLEAR (in capitals, the word for a night when every lamp can be seen from the breakwater) → “晴明”, the same in the text and in note 6.

Units and dates stay as in the source (forty miles → 四十英里; the ninth of March, 1952 → 一九五二年三月九日; 1871 → 一八七一年; "a quarter past six" → 六点一刻). Chapter titles: "Chapter One The Harbour at Dusk" → "第一章 黄昏的港口" (chapter number in Chinese, one half-width space, title).

<p id="2">“You must understand,” he said to me on my first evening, “that a lamp is not for the man who lights it. It is for the man who is still out there.” He pointed with his chin toward the grey line where the sea met the sky. “If I forget that, I might as well stay home.”</p>
