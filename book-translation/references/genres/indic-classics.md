# Indian epics, Puranas and other Sanskrit classics in English translation

A tested convention set, from translating several Puranas (Debroy's *Shiva Purana*, the Motilal AITM series, the Kashiraj *Vāmana Purāṇa*). Paste the block below into the book's `style.md`, after the paragraph describing the edition, and adjust names to the book. It also serves as a model for other traditions (Chinese Buddhist convention for Pali texts, established 汉译 for Greek and Latin classics): the same headings, different reference translations.

---

The result should read like a published Chinese translation of an Indian epic — the register of 黄宝生 / 金克木 译《摩诃婆罗多》 and 季羡林 译《罗摩衍那》散文部分 — not like a machine rendering of English. 庄重、典雅、流畅，带古典叙事的色彩，但不是文言，也不是白话小说腔。可以用「曰」「乃」「遂」「此」一类词点缀，不可通篇文言。

**What the text is.** A Purana is a framed oral narrative. A narrator recounts to sages what another narrator told him, who recounts what a god said, and so on, several frames deep. The prose is formulaic and repetitive by design: vocatives (“O Suta!”, “O lords among sages!”), refrains of praise, lists of rituals and rewards, hymns. Keep all of this. The repetition and formulae are the texture of a Purana; do not smooth, compress, or “improve” them. If the source says “You are blessed. You are blessed.” the Chinese says it twice.

**Pronouns.** Gods, sages and male characters 他; Devi, Sati, Parvati and female characters 她; abstract entities, animals and objects 它. Brahman (the absolute) is 它 or is rephrased to avoid a pronoun.

**Vocatives and courtesy.** 呼格（“O Suta!”, “O immensely fortunate one!”, “O tiger among sages!”）是往世书对话的标志，必须保留，并译得自然：“苏多啊！”“大福之人啊！”“众仙中的猛虎啊！”。同一段里连续多个呼格，可以在每个后面都用“啊”，也可以合并为一个“啊”，以读来顺口为准。“O father!” 译“父亲啊！”（对长辈仙人的敬称，照译即可）。敬语与谦语：“Please tell us” → “请为我们讲说”；“I prostrate myself before” → “我顶礼”；“show us your favours” → “垂恩于我们”。

**Hymns and lists.** 赞颂、颂歌（stotra）和排比段落：用短句和对仗的节奏，可以适当四字、六字并置，不押韵，不加原文没有的意象。列举仪轨、报偿、罪业的段落：保持清单式的清晰，不为了文采改写。

**Names and epithets.** A god has many names (Shiva is also Shankara, Shambhu, Mahadeva, Maheshvara, Hara, Rudra, Ishana; Vishnu is also Hari, Narayana, Keshava; Brahma is also Vidhatri, Prajapati, Svayambhu), and each keeps its own rendering from the glossary. Never collapse two of them into one name, and never substitute the god's commonest name for an epithet the source chose. For a name not in the glossary, transliterate in the Buddhist-Indological convention (那罗陀, 迦叶波, 补罗斯底耶 — characters like 罗 陀 迦 婆 那 摩 耶 毗 阇 阿 尼 吒 娑 舍 提 多 达 曼 底), not the modern news-agency style (纳拉达, 卡什亚帕). If a character or place already has an established Chinese name from the Mahabharata / Ramayana translations or from Buddhist texts, use it. Text names: 《摩诃婆罗多》, 吠陀, 奥义书 (with the specific name: 《歌者奥义书》 for Chandogya); 《》 for titles.

**Glossary conventions** (for the terminology pass). Established names take precedence: 湿婆, 毗湿奴, 梵天, 因陀罗, 阿耆尼, 伐楼那, 阎摩, 俱毗罗, 迦楼罗, 那罗陀, 毗耶娑, 达刹, 迦叶波, 婆利古, 阿修罗, 罗刹, 夜叉, 乾闼婆, 紧那罗, 天女 (apsara), 迦尸, 迦梨, 雪山 (Himalaya), 恒河, 弥卢山 (Meru), 凯拉萨山 (Kailasa — not 冈仁波齐). Shiva's epithets each keep their own rendering: Shankara 商羯罗, Shambhu 商布, Mahadeva 大天, Maheshvara 大自在天, Hara 诃罗, Rudra 楼陀罗, Ishana 伊舍那, Shulapani 持三叉戟者, Nilakantha 青颈者, Pashupati 兽主, Trilochana 三眼者, Chandrashekhara 戴月者, Girisha 山主. Vishnu: Hari 诃利, Narayana 那罗延, Keshava 计舍婆, Janardana 遮那陀那, Madhava 摩陀婆. Brahma: Vidhatri 造物主, Prajapati 生主, Svayambhu 自生者. Devi: Uma 乌玛, Sati 萨蒂, Parvati 帕尔瓦蒂, Jagadamba 世界之母, Kali 迦梨. Descriptive epithets are translated; name-like epithets are transliterated. Mark `no_romanize: true` for naturalized words: 吠陀, 婆罗门, 刹帝利, 吠舍, 首陀罗, 瑜伽, 业 (karma), 法 (dharma), 劫, 由旬, 阿修罗, 罗刹, 夜叉, 乾闼婆, 天女, 仙人 (rishi), 苦行, 曼陀罗 (mantra), 林伽.

**Sanskrit terms in italics.** The source keeps Sanskrit words in italics (`*term*`, plurals `*term*s`). Render each with its glossary Chinese. On the FIRST occurrence of a given italic term in the chunk, append the romanized Sanskrit in full-width parentheses: 林伽（linga）, 劫（kalpa）. Later occurrences: Chinese only. `no_romanize` terms never get the parenthesis. Never romanize proper names.

## Edition-specific additions seen so far

- **OCR'd editions** (diacritics unreliable: Ganeśa / Ganesa / Gaņeśa are one name; italics lost, so Sanskrit terms are recognized by diacritics, and still romanized on first use).
- **Verse numbers** at paragraph starts (`1.`, `4-5.`, `38-41ab.`, `41cd-47.`): reproduce exactly, then a space, then the Chinese: `4-5. 即使他……`.
- **Speaker lines** (`*The bard said:*`): keep the asterisks, translate the name, full-width colon: `*吟诵者说：*`. Never romanize in a speaker line.
- **Chapter titles** like `Chapter 5 'Conversation between Sudharmā and Cyavana'` → `第5章（苏达尔玛与奇耶婆那的对话）`; Debroy's `Chapter 1-1(1) (Greatness of Shiva Purana)` keeps its numeric code as is.
- **A different edition of the same tradition** romanizes differently (Debroy's Shiva, Daksha vs Motilal's Śiva, Dakṣa): seed the glossary with `seed_glossary.py --from <earlier-book> --to <new-book>`; it carried 276 of 635 names across for one book.
